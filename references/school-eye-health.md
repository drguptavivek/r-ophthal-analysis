# School Eye Health Programmes

Load this for school vision screening, programme monitoring, spectacle provision and wear, and school-based trials. Refractive error definitions and two-stage screening designs are in `myopia.md`; school-age eREC in `who-ecim-erec-formulas.md`; screening accuracy methods in `clinical-diagnostic-prediction.md`.

## Screening Protocol (WHO 2025 Handbook)

- Test each eye separately, with spectacles removed, on a 6/12 line (Tumbling E, HOTV, LEA or letter chart, or the WHOeyes app).
- Pass: more than half the symbols correct on the 6/12 line (3 of 4 or 3 of 5). Refer: fewer than that on the 6/60 or 6/12 line in one or both eyes.
- Also refer for: reported diabetes; eye pain, discomfort or severe itch; crusting, pus or eyelid swelling; excessive discharge; abnormal red eye; haziness of iris, pupil or cornea; misaligned eyes; untestable or uncooperative child. Any eye injury is an urgent referral.
- IAPB: start at age 5-6 years and revisit schools every 1-2 years; each year screen the new intake and re-examine every child given spectacles the year before.

Monocular distance VA screening misses most hyperopia and astigmatism; a plus-lens test (for example +1.50 D in REACH) or refraction is needed to detect them. State the screening chart, line, pass rule, screener cadre, and confirmatory test.

## Screener Models

Teacher, community health worker or nurse, eye care professional, school-based refraction with on-site spectacles, and referral-based models differ in accuracy, cost and follow-up (Little 2025). Teacher screening sensitivity ranges from 25% to 94% with generally high specificity. Report screener cadre, training and refresher schedule, and accuracy against a professional reference on a sample.

## Programme Indicators

Report as numerator / denominator x 100, disaggregated by gender and age (5-10, over 10 years) at least (WHO 2025, Table 1):

| Indicator | Denominator |
| --- | --- |
| Children screened | Children of that age in the target population |
| Refer result for vision or eye problems | Children screened |
| Diagnosed VI or blindness by eye care personnel | Children screened |
| Prescribed and fitted with spectacles | Children screened |
| Referral uptake (reached eye care) | Children referred |
| Spectacle wear at follow-up | Children given spectacles |
| School-age eREC | Children with refractive error need (`who-ecim-erec-formulas.md`) |

More than half of children who fail screening often never reach eye care (Little 2025), so referral uptake must be measured, not assumed; track it with referral slips, a hospital register or mobile follow-up.

## Spectacle Wear Outcome

- Measure at an unannounced school visit by masked field workers, at a prespecified time after dispensing (3-4 months in Morjaria 2017 and 2020; also 6 months and 1 year).
- Classify each child: wearing at the visit; not wearing but had them at school; not wearing, reported at home; lost or broken. The primary outcome is observed wearing; report the other categories so "possession" is not counted as wear.
- Self-report overstates wear; use it only as a secondary measure.

## Outcome Definitions Adopted Across Studies

Definitions vary so much that results are rarely comparable (Morjaria 2019 review of 35 studies: compliance from 13.4% to 87.4%). Prespecify each outcome with all of the elements below, and report a second definition as sensitivity when comparing with other studies.

| Outcome | Variants in use | Adopt by default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spectacle compliance | Wearing (16 studies); wearing or carrying (7); purchased or self-reported; not stated (4) | Observed wearing; carrying reported separately |
| How assessed | Unannounced observation (15 studies); planned visit (2); self-report (rest) | Unannounced, masked observer; self-report secondary |
| Timing | 1 to 18 months after dispensing | Fixed interval (3-4 or 6 months), stated |
| Denominator | Prescribed, dispensed, or present at follow-up | Dispensed; report loss to follow-up as its own category |
| Non-compliance reasons | Lost, broken, headache or watering, teasing, parental disapproval (Pawar 2023, assessed 3-6 months after dispensing) | Prespecified reason list, one primary reason |
| Screening accuracy | Screener vs professional on referred children only, or on a random sample of passes too | Validate both: all referrals plus a random sample of passes (Shukla 2018: optometrist rechecked 5% of teacher passes; teacher sensitivity 92.3%) |
| Referral uptake | Attended, examined, or treated, at varying times | Examined by eye care within a stated period |
| Spectacle coverage (cohort) | Among all children with refractive error, met plus newly corrected | Gupta 2019 model: refractive error = unaided VA 6/12 or worse in either eye with BCVA 6/9.5 or better in both; met need = already wearing correct spectacles (PVA 6/9.5 or better in both eyes); unmet need = the rest; coverage observed directly at baseline and at an unannounced 1-year visit; paired change tested with McNemar; report separately for baseline wearers and newly provided |

Self-reported and observed wear diverge within the same studies (Morjaria 2019), so never pool the two.

## School-Based Trials

- Randomise schools (clusters); report the number of clusters, ICC, and analyse with cluster-adjusted methods (mixed models or GEE, or cluster-level summaries when clusters are few).
- Prespecify the margin: superiority (Morjaria 2020: 20 percentage points, 50 schools) or non-inferiority (ready-made versus custom spectacles, Morjaria 2017).
- Educational outcomes: standardised test scores (report in SD units), grade repetition, dropout; specify the test, timing and masking.
- Report contamination between schools, spectacle replacement policy, and costs per child screened and per child corrected.

## Evidence Notes (for framing, not as defaults)

Free spectacles increase wear compared with a prescription alone, and health education alone has not improved wear (Cochrane review, Evans 2018, as summarised in later reviews); spectacle provision improved test scores in several trials (Little 2025). Verify effect sizes in the primary reviews before citing.
